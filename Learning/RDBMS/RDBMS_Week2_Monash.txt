===================WEEK 02===================
The Database lifecycle consists of the following phases: Requirements Definition, Conceptual Design, Logical Design and Physical Design
In the Requirements Definition phase, we get a statement of specifications which describes the user views' particular requirements and constraints.

The conceptual design is represented as an ERD - Entity Relationship Diagram.
NOTE: When we make a Conceptual Design using these rules, we DO NOT decide the type of database (RDB or Otherwise) to be used.
NOTE: The Carlos M. Coronel Textbook makes a very poor distinction between Conceptual Model and Logical Model.

An ENTITY will have attributes (things we wish to record), one or more of which will identify an entity instance (called the KEY).
NOTE: At the Conceptual Model stage, there is no Primary Key or Foreign Key, just a Key.

After constructing the Conceptual Model, we need to construct the Logical Model which targets a type of Database. 
In our case, this is a Relational Database, and hence, will be a Relational Model.

NOTE: We will be using the Crow's Foot Notation (CFN) here.
The CFN speaks of Connectivity and Cardinality. 
Connectivity refers to the way in which 2 given Entities connect with each other.
Eg - Entity A Supports 1 of Entity B and Entity B supports 1 of Entity A. (1 to 1)
Eg - Entity A Supports N of Entity B and Entity B supports 1 of Entity A. (1 to N)
Eg - Entity A Supports N of Entity B and Entity B supports M of Entity A. (M to N)

Eg - A(1,1) - B(0, N) -> Here the min. Cardinality of A is 1 and the max. Cardinality of A is also 1. Here the min. Cardinality of B is 0 and the max. Cardinality of B is N.

There are 3 Degrees of relationships: Unary, Binary and Ternary.  
A Unary Relationship is an Entity having a relationship with itself.
A Binary Relationship is a relationship between 2 Entities.
A Ternary Relationship is a Relationship between 3 or more Entities.

NOTE: We will NOT BE USING Strong and Weak Relationships.

A Weak Entity is an Entity that has a key that requires the existence of one or more other entities, i.e., its key will have a key from the Strong Entity.
Eg - FAMILY Entity. The Family Entity cannot exist without the Employee Entity. 

Eg - Family member 1 of Employee 1. 
Eg - Family member 2 of Employee 1. 
Eg - Family member 1 of Employee 2. 
Eg - Family member 2 of Employee 2.

A Strong Entity is an Entity that has a key that can be defined without reference to other entities.
Eg - EMPLOYEE Entity.
Weak and Strong Entities are decided using Business Rules.

Identifying vs. Non-Identifying relationship.

Identifying -> In an Identifying relationship, Identifier of A is part of Identifier of B, i.e., the key of the child is part of the key of the parent. It is shown by a solid line. Eg -> ENROLMENT - STUDENT. The student_id is included in ENROLMENT's Key.

Non-Identifying Relationship -> In a Non-Identifying relationship, Identifier of A is NOT part of Identifier of B. It is shown by a dotted line. Eg -> DEPARTMENT - EMPLOYEE. The dept_no is not part of the EMPLOYEE's identifier.

Types of Attributes:
Single -> Can't be subdivided.
Composite -> Can be divided into additional attributes.
Single-Valued -> Can only have a Single value.


Multi-Valued -> Can have many values.
Derived -> Can be derived from an algorithm.

MONASH SOFTWARE CASE STUDY
  * EMPLOYEE (emp_id, emp_full_name, emp_address, emp_dob, emp_tfn, team_leader)
  * TEAM     (team_id, team_name, member_id)
  * FAMILY   (family_id, family_member_num)
  * TRAINING (train_code, train_name, train_completion_date)  

NOTE: Team Leader isn't an Entity since it is just an EMPLOYEE acting in a certain role. 
NOTE: A Mentor isn't an Entity since it is just an EMPLOYEE acting in a certain role.
  
MY SOLUTION:
* An EMPLOYEE can belong to 0 or 1 TEAM. A TEAM can have N EMPLOYEE. EMPLOYEE - TEAM (N - 1).
* An EMPLOYEE can have 1 or more SKILLS. Each SKILL belongs to 1 EMPLOYEE. [SKILLS is a Relation since it is a multi-valued attribute]

* An EMPLOYEE can LeAd a TEAM (This is how we represent the second relationship between EMPLOYEE and TEAM). 
* An EMPLOYEE can have 0 or 1 MENTOR. Every MENTOR can have 1 or N EMPLOYEE. EMPLOYEE - MENTOR (1 to 0 or 1).

* An EMPLOYEE can complete 0 or more TRAINING Programs. A TRAINING Program can be completed by 1 or more EMPLOYEE. EMPLOYEE - TRAIN (1 or N - 0 or N).
* An EMPLOYEE can 0 or more FAMILY members that have benefits. Each of these FAMILY members can belong to one EMPLOYEE. EMPLOYEE - FAMILY (1 - 0 or N).

Lindsay Smith's Solution:
* The Entity Name should always be a Singular, never a Plural.
* Established the TEAM, TRAINING, EMPLOYEE and FAMILY_MEMBER entities.

* Should establish the relations between them.
* Team Leader isn't an Entity since it is just an EMPLOYEE acting in a certain role.

* A Mentor isn't an Entity since it is just an EMPLOYEE acting in a certain role.
* Address is a composite attribute.

* A multi-valued attribute is dealt with by moving it out into a new Entity. This new Entity will be a Weak Entity and its Key will depend on (have an attribute from) the Parent's Key.

* The EMPLOYEE - TRAINING relationship is (0, N) - (0, M) since EMPLOYEE DO NOT have to mandatorily take up TRAINING. It is also because a particular TRAINING can be taken up by M EMPLOYEE.
  NOTE: Here we assume that a TRAINING can exist even if it has 0 EMPLOYEE in it.  

* Now, we need to look at the TRAINING_CERTIFICATE Entity. A TRAINING_CERTIFICATE cannot be stored in an EMPLOYEE Entity since a given EMPLOYEE may complete N TRAININGS and this would result in TRAINING_CERTIFICATE being a multi-valued attribute. A TRAINING_CERTIFICATE cannot be stored in a TRAINING either since a given TRAINING may be completed by N EMPLOYEE. A given EMPLOYEE may also complete a given TRAINING more than once. Thus we need to create a new Entity called TRAINING_CERTIFICATE which will have the emp_no and train_code keys. This type of entity is called an Associative Entity.

* The Associative Entity will have N:1 (N being on the Associative Entity's side) relationship with both its parent Entities.
